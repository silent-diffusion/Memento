# Memento user guide

Memento records meetings, interviews, lectures and dictation on your Windows PC, keeps each sound source as its own track, writes down what was said on your PC, tells the speakers apart, turns a recording into minutes, summaries and other documents, and lets you export exactly the parts you need. Nothing you record leaves your PC unless you export it yourself or choose an online AI service for a document.

This guide covers version 2.0.0. Features new in 2.0 are video ([Recording video](#recording-video)), the live transcript ([During the recording](#during-the-recording)), suggested chapters and selecting lines ([Review and the transcript](#review-and-the-transcript)), remembering speakers by voice ([Speakers](#speakers)), Tesseract ([Agenda and attachments](#agenda-and-attachments)), the follow-up email and the preview sheet ([Documents](#documents)), video export ([Export](#export)), and the tray, starting with Windows and Keep only the mix ([Settings](#settings)).

## Install

1. Open the [latest release](https://github.com/silent-diffusion/Memento/releases/latest) on GitHub.
2. Download `MementoApp-win-Setup.exe`.
3. Run it. Memento installs for your Windows account only, needs no administrator rights, and starts when it is done. If the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime is missing, Setup installs it first.

You need Windows 11, or Windows 10 version 2004 or later, on a 64-bit PC. A graphics card makes transcription, the local AI model and video encoding much faster but is not required. On Windows 10 a screen recording always shows Windows' yellow border around what is captured; only Windows 11 lets Memento turn it off.

The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you the first time. If you downloaded it from the project's Releases page, choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

To remove Memento, use **Settings › Apps › Installed apps** in Windows. Uninstalling removes the program but keeps your recordings, settings and models (see [Where your data lives](#where-your-data-lives-and-how-to-back-it-up)).

## Your first recording

1. Start Memento. The Library opens; on the first run it is empty.
2. Choose **Start your first recording** (later: **New recording** at the top right).
3. Type a title over "Untitled meeting", or leave it. The type next to it (Meeting, Interview, Lecture, Dictation or one of your own) only helps you find recordings later.
4. Check the sources on the left: your microphone and "Everything this PC plays" are on by default. The level bars move when there is sound.
5. Press **Start recording**. It starts at once.
6. When you are done, press the round **Stop** button. Review opens while Memento saves the tracks, then the transcript is made on your PC.

Transcription needs a model. The first time, Memento shows where to install one: **Settings › Transcription**, where each model shows its size before you download it. Large v3 Turbo is recommended with a graphics card, Small without one. A recording made before a model was installed is transcribed as soon as it is.

## Choosing what to record

The **Audio sources** list on the Record screen offers:

- **Microphones**: every microphone Windows knows, including headsets.
- **Everything this PC plays**: the sound of your speakers or headphones, for example the other people in a video call.
- **Single apps**: one program at a time, such as Zoom, Teams or a browser. Only that program's sound is recorded, not notifications or music from other programs. An app appears in the list once it has played sound; press **Add** to look again.

Each source you turn on becomes its own track, and all tracks stay in sync. A typical call is your microphone plus the call app (or plus "Everything this PC plays"). Memento remembers your last choice.

The laptop microphone also hears your speakers. That is fine: both tracks are kept and transcribed. Use headphones if you want the microphone track to hold only your own voice.

## During the recording

- The big timer shows how long has been recorded. The footer says when the last checkpoint was saved: every 30 seconds by default, everything recorded so far is on disk and would survive a crash or power cut.
- **Pause** stops writing without ending the recording; **Resume** continues. The gap is remembered, so transcript times stay right.
- **Mark highlight** (or Ctrl+M) marks the moment; type a note if you like. Highlights show in Review and attach to the transcript line.
- Turn a source on or off at any time; the other tracks are not affected.
- If a source is much quieter than the others, or its speech stays under −30 dB, a line under its meter says so: "Microphone is quiet (−38 dB). Move closer or raise its level in Windows; the transcript may miss you." It looks at the last 10 seconds, so it appears and goes calmly and only while someone speaks into that source. Memento already transcribes every source at an even level, but a voice barely above the room noise can still be missed.
- **Details and agenda** opens the details: participants, purpose, platform, and an agenda you can tick off as you cover it.
- If a device disappears (a headset is unplugged, a microphone is disabled), only its track stops. A message names the device and the time, and the other sources keep recording. Turn the source on again when the device is back to continue it as a new track.
- Keep the PC from sleeping while you record: nothing can be heard while it sleeps. If it does sleep, the time it slept is kept as silence on every track, so the tracks stay in step with each other.
- If the drive runs low on space, a banner says how much is left; recording continues and transcription waits. If the drive fills up, the recording stops at a stated time and everything up to then is kept.

Transcription and other work never slow a recording down: while you record, they wait (if **Pause when the PC is busy** is on, which it is by default).

**Live transcript.** With **Live transcript while recording** on (Settings › Transcription; off by default), the Live transcript card shows a rough draft a few seconds behind the recording, made on this PC by the Small model (or Base). It is a draft: it is not saved, it has no speakers, and the full transcript made after you stop replaces it. It uses the processor unless you let it use the graphics card, never takes more than a quarter of the processor threads, and steps aside while Memento transcribes another recording or when a document needs the graphics card; the card says so. If neither Small nor Base is installed, the card says to install one.

**Resuming a stopped recording.** Stopped too early? While the recording is being saved, press **Resume recording** on the Recording screen: as soon as it is saved, Memento records more into the same recording with the same sources. Later the same day, open the recording and choose **Resume recording** in Review's header. What was recorded before is kept exactly as it was; the new part is added after it, the time in between shows as a pause on the waveform ("Resumed at 10:42 AM · paused 12 min"), the mix covers both, and the transcript and the other steps run again over the whole recording (the earlier transcript stays until the new one replaces it). Only recordings started today can be resumed, and not imported ones or ones that keep only their mix. There is no undo: press Stop to end the new part. If Memento closes during it, the next start repairs that part like any recording.

## Recording video

Memento can record a display, one window or a camera beside the audio. Video is always one more track in step with the sound, never the recording itself: if it stops, the audio carries on.

- **Turn it on** in the **Video** section of the Record screen (or make Audio + video the recording mode in Settings › Recording). **Screen** captures a whole display: everything shown on it, other windows and notifications included; your other displays are not captured. **Window** captures one window even when others cover it; if it is minimised the video pauses and continues when it comes back. **Camera** records a webcam or a virtual camera. With a screen and a camera, **Screen + camera** shows the camera as a small picture in a corner you choose; both are kept as separate tracks, so you can move the picture later in Review.
- **Virtual cameras work too**: the OBS Virtual Camera, NDI, Snap Camera, capture cards and other software cameras that are not listed as Windows cameras appear in the camera list with the note "via DirectShow" and record like a webcam. For OBS, choose **Start Virtual Camera** in OBS first: until OBS sends a picture the thumbnail shows OBS's placeholder and Memento says the camera "is not started in OBS". If OBS stops its virtual camera (or closes) while you record, the camera track ends there with everything before it kept, and the screen and audio carry on; start the virtual camera again and turn the camera back on to start a new camera track.
- **A camera is missing from the list?** Memento lists every camera Windows and DirectShow know when the Record screen opens and again every few seconds. Connect the camera (or start the app that provides a virtual camera, and its virtual camera), wait a moment, and it appears. If it still does not, check that the camera works in the Windows Camera app and that Settings › Privacy & security › Camera allows desktop apps; a virtual camera must be installed in its 64-bit version (Memento is a 64-bit app; OBS's installer adds both).
- **You can change what is captured while recording**: pick another display or window and the same track continues. Turning a source off ends its track; turning it on again starts a new one from that moment.
- Memento's own window is listed but cannot be chosen, and windows that keep themselves out of recordings (protected video players, some password managers) are refused rather than recorded as black.
- The **Encoder** card shows whether the graphics card or the processor encodes, the resolution, the frame rate with any dropped frames, the bitrate and how much video has been written. If the graphics card's encoder fails, recording continues with the processor's and a banner says so.
- **When something goes wrong** the video stops first and alone: a disconnected display pauses its video (switch to another display or wait for it), a closed window or unplugged camera ends its track with everything before it kept, a camera another app holds or Windows' camera privacy switch is named with the fix, and when the disk runs low (below 1 GB) the video stops so the audio can keep going.
- **Space**: screen recordings are mostly still, so they usually take 0.6 to 2.3 GB per hour at 1080p or 1440p; busy video can take up to about 5 GB per hour at Balanced. Settings › Recording › Video defaults sets the resolution (match the display, 1080p or 720p), frame rate (24, 30 or 60) and quality (Smaller, Balanced, Best).
- After you stop, the **Video** stage checks the files (and joins them if the encoder changed while recording). It never holds the transcript back. In Review the video plays above the waveform in step with the transcript; clicking a line seeks the video too.
- **Remove video, keep audio** (Review, or Settings › Storage and history › Remove video older than) deletes the video files to free space after asking; the audio, transcript and documents stay, and the recording's History says when and how much was freed. It cannot be undone.

Video is never transcribed, analysed or sent anywhere: only the audio tracks are transcribed.

## Review and the transcript

Review opens after you stop, or when you open a recording from the Library.

- The **player** plays the mix of all tracks. Click the waveform to jump, or use the time slider (left/right arrows: 5 seconds; with Shift: 30 seconds). **Skip silences** (next to the speed) jumps over every pause longer than a second and a half between transcript lines; the skipped stretches are dimmed on the waveform and "Skipped 4 s" appears briefly where playback landed. It needs a transcript, never skips while you scrub, and Memento remembers whether it is on.
- On a window 1024 pixels or wider the player stays in place and the outline on the left, the transcript and the details on the right each scroll on their own. On a narrower window they stack and the page scrolls.
- The **transcript** follows the playback. Click a line's time (or anywhere beside its words) to play it. Click its words to correct them: the line becomes a text box with the cursor where you clicked; **Enter** or clicking elsewhere saves ("Saved" appears next to Undo), **Esc** cancels. Double-click and F2 still edit too, and the transcript does not scroll away from a line you are correcting while it plays. The original wording is kept. Words the engine was unsure of have a dotted underline.
- **Search** finds words in the transcript; the Library search also finds words across all recordings.
- **Filter the transcript.** Click a speaker's name under **People** to see only their lines; the name stays pressed in and shows how many lines are theirs. Click it again, choose **Show all**, or press **Esc** to see every line. Ctrl+click a second name to see both speakers. **Filter**, above the transcript, combines more: several speakers, lines with a highlight, lines with uncertain words, lines you corrected, the lines of one chapter, and the lines matching your search. A line above the transcript says what you are looking at ("Showing 42 of 318 lines · Sarah · Highlights"). The other lines are only hidden: playing, correcting a line, the arrow keys and Undo all work on the lines shown. The filter lasts while the recording is open.
- **Copy** the transcript from the **⋯** menu (**As text** or **As Markdown**), or the lines a filter shows with **Copy** next to **Show all**; paste it anywhere. "Copied 42 of 318 lines as text" appears next to Undo. Memento puts it on the Windows clipboard itself, and tells Windows not to sync it to your other devices.
- **Mark as reviewed** when you have checked it.
- **Chapters and highlights** are on the left; add chapters by hand at the playback position. Click a time to play from there; click a name to rename it in place (Enter saves, Esc cancels); × removes it. A highlight's note under its transcript line can be renamed the same way. Topics are suggested from the words on your PC.
- **Undo** (in the header, or **Ctrl+Z**) takes back what you just did in Review: a corrected line, a speaker moved, added, renamed or merged, a highlight, chapter or topic added, renamed or removed, a renamed person, tags, the reviewed mark. **Ctrl+Y** or **Ctrl+Shift+Z** does it again. The button's tooltip names the step ("Undo merge speakers") and "Undone: merge speakers" appears beside it. Up to 50 steps are kept while the recording is open; opening another recording starts afresh.
- **Details** and **History** are on the right. History lists everything that happened to the recording: when it was recorded, saved, transcribed, recovered, and why anything failed.
- **Suggested chapters.** Once a recording longer than six minutes is transcribed, **Suggested chapters** under **Chapters** lists where the subject seems to change, worked out on your PC from the words, pauses and changes of speaker (nothing is sent anywhere). The titles have a dotted underline until you accept them: the check mark accepts one, × dismisses it for good, **Accept all** takes them all; click a time to listen first. Accepted suggestions are ordinary chapters you can rename or remove, and Undo takes any of these back. The titles are keywords from what was said, so expect to rename some.
- **Select several lines.** Ctrl+click a line to select it (a checkbox appears on every line); Shift+click selects every line from the last one you clicked; **Ctrl+A** on a line selects every line shown (with a filter on, only those). While selecting, a click selects or clears a line, Space does the same for the focused line, and Shift+↑/↓ grows or shrinks the selection. The bar above the lines says how many are selected and acts on all of them: **Assign speaker…** (when they all belong to one speaker you can also merge that speaker), **Highlight**, **Mark as chapter start** (at the first selected line), **Copy** and **Remove highlights**. **Cancel** or **Esc** stops selecting. Each action is one Undo step.
- **Right-click a line** (or press **Shift+F10** or the menu key on it) for the same actions on that line, plus **Play from** and **Select**; on a selected line the menu acts on the whole selection.
- **Go back to an earlier version from History.** A History line whose result was kept (transcribed, speakers identified, a line corrected, speakers merged, a document generated or edited) is underlined: click it to see the transcript as it was right after that, in place of the current one, under a banner such as "Transcript as of 10:42 AM, after speakers were identified". It is read-only; the player keeps playing and the line being heard is marked. **Restore this version** makes it the transcript again (the one you had is kept as a version, and **Undo** brings it back); **Back to current** or **Esc** closes it. A document line opens that version in the document viewer the same way. Lines that changed nothing you can open (recorded, saved, exported, failures), and moments of which no copy was kept (a run of corrections is kept once, and versions go after the days set in **Settings › Documents**), are not underlined; hovering one says why. This needs version history on, which it is unless you turned it off.
- **Line times follow the voice.** Each line is highlighted from the moment its words start, also after long pauses, and silences in the recording no longer confuse the transcription (before 1.3.0 a line could light up a few seconds before it was said, and after a long silence the engine could repeat one sentence instead of transcribing). Recordings transcribed before keep their times until you transcribe them again from the More menu.
- **Video.** A recording with video shows it above the waveform: the screen with the camera picture, or the camera alone. It follows the one playhead: playing, skipping, clicking a transcript line, Skip silences and the speed all move the picture with the sound. **Camera inset** hides or shows the camera picture and its corner buttons move it while you watch (the recording is not changed); **Audio only** hides the video; **Fullscreen** fills the centre with it (Esc returns). The outline lists the video's sources, resolution, frame rate and size, with **Remove video, keep audio…**. Once removed, Review says when and how much it freed. A recording without video looks as before.
- Where a track had speech but the transcript has nothing for ten seconds or more, Review says so at that place and offers **Transcribe again** with another model. You can also transcribe the whole recording again with another model or language from the More menu; with version history on, the earlier transcript is kept and can be restored from Details.

If transcription fails, Review says what happened, what was kept (a partial transcript is never thrown away) and offers the most specific fix first, such as **Retry on CPU** or **Use the Small model**. **Details** opens the History.

## Speakers

After the transcript, Memento tells the voices apart and labels them Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on.

- Rename a speaker once and every line changes.
- Click the speaker name on a line to move that line to another speaker. The menu opens with a **Find or add a speaker** field: type part of a name (capitals and accents do not matter) and the list narrows to the speakers who match, names that start with what you typed first; the arrow keys move through it, Enter picks, Esc closes. When nobody has the name you typed, the last row reads **Add "{name}" as a new speaker**: it adds the speaker and gives them the line. A long list scrolls inside the menu.
- After you pick another speaker, the menu asks how, and says how many lines each choice moves: **Just this line** moves only that line; **This turn** moves the run of lines by this speaker around it (the whole thing they said before someone else spoke); **From here on** moves this line and every later line of this speaker, which suits correcting from the top down; when the two names are simply the wrong way round, **Swap {old} and {new} from here on** trades the lines of both speakers from this line to the end, and **Swap {old} and {new} everywhere** trades every line of both (the names and colours stay where they are); **Merge {old} into {new}** moves every line of the old speaker to the new one, and the old speaker goes away. Point at a choice (or move to it with the arrow keys) and the lines it would move light up in the transcript. Esc goes back to the list. Undo takes any of them back in one step.
- People you named are always listed first (in the People list, the speaker menu and its search), then the unnamed "Speaker n" ones, each in the order they first speak.
- **Merge** a speaker into another from the People list; its menu has the same search. Undo brings a merged speaker back with its name, colour and lines.
- **Who spoke**: tell Memento how many people spoke, and their names if you know them, for this recording: in **Details and agenda** on the Record screen (before or during the recording), in **Edit details**, or under **People** in Review. **Use participants** takes the participants as the names. Memento then groups the voices it hears into that many speakers, on every track, and names them in the order they first speak. Without a number of its own, a recording uses **Expected speakers** from **Settings › Speakers**; with neither, Memento decides by how the voices sound. While recording, press **Speaking now** beside a name in **Details and agenda** while that person is talking (as often as you like): the voice heard at those moments is named after them when the recording is processed, before the remaining names go by the order of first speaking. The × beside the count clears a name's marks.
- Too many speakers? Under **People**, set **Who spoke** to the right number and choose **Reduce to {n} speakers**: the speakers whose voices sound most alike are merged, named speakers stay apart, and Undo puts them back in one step. Or choose **Identify speakers again**: the speakers are grouped again from what Memento heard (in seconds when nothing else changed); the lines you corrected keep their speaker, and the voices under them guide the rest (a voice you put on Ana is never grouped with one you put on Ben, so one corrected line per voice fixes every line of that voice); names you gave are kept; and (with version history on) the transcript as it was is kept as a version you can restore from **Details**.
- **Add from speakers** in Edit details adds the named speakers to the participants (never "Speaker 2"); Undo takes them out again.
- Speaker identification runs on the processor and needs the speaker models from **Settings › Speakers**.
- **Split lines where the speaker changes** (in **Settings › Speakers**, on by default): a line that holds two people's words ("Yes. — So the budget…") is cut where the voice changes, so each part gets its own speaker. It applies when a transcript is first made; lines you edited, and transcripts whose speakers are identified again, keep their lines as they are. History says how many lines were split.
- **Remember speakers by voice** (in **Settings › Speakers**, off until you turn it on): when you name a speaker in Review, Memento keeps a voice signature for that name, a short list of numbers from the voice model, never the sound. In later recordings, an unnamed speaker who sounds like someone you named shows "Sounds like Sam Okafor · 91% match · 4 past recordings" under it in **People**: **Use name** names them (and teaches Memento a little more about the voice), **Not Sam** hides the suggestion for this recording. Memento never names anyone by itself, and Undo takes either back. Names in **Who spoke** and the participants are suggested a little more readily. **Known voices** in Settings lists every learned voice; switch off **Suggest** to keep a voice without it being suggested, **Forget** removes one at once and **Forget all voices…** removes them all. Deleting a recording also removes what was learned from it. The signatures stay on your PC, in the library's `voices` folder.

## Agenda and attachments

Open **Details and agenda** (while recording) or the Details tab in Review, then drop a file on the agenda area, choose one, or paste the text. Memento reads Word, PDF, Excel, CSV, Markdown and plain text files, and photos or scans of a printed agenda, all on your PC. Photos and scanned PDFs are read with Windows' own text recognition, or with **Tesseract**, an open-source alternative you can install in **Settings › Documents › Agenda photos and scans** (4 MB, downloaded once and checked). Tesseract marks the words it was unsure of; Windows does not say how sure it is, so check its items against the photo. The caption under **Agenda** says which one read it.

Items Memento is unsure of are marked with the reason, for example a heading that may have run into the next item. Fix them in the list, reorder them, then press **Done**. While recording, tick items off as you cover them. The original file is kept with the recording as an attachment; you can add other files too (up to 100 MB each).

## Importing audio or video

**Import audio or video** (in the Library's More menu, or on the first-run screen) turns an existing file into a recording: WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A, WMA, OGG and more, and the sound track of a video file. It is stored, transcribed and given speakers like any other recording. The original file is neither copied elsewhere nor changed.

## The Library: folders, dates and deleting audio

**Timeline and Folders.** The switch at the top of the Library chooses how it is shown. **Timeline** lists every recording by date, as before. **Folders** shows a folder tree beside the list: **Library** holds the recordings in no folder, and each folder shows how many recordings it holds (with its own folders). Click a folder to list its recordings; search and the type buttons still work inside it. Memento remembers which view you used last.

- **Make a folder** with **+** at the top of the tree, or **New folder inside…** in a folder's **⋯** menu. Folders can hold folders, eight levels deep. A folder's **⋯** menu also has **Rename…**, **Move to…** and **Delete…**.
- **Put a recording in a folder** by dragging its row onto the folder, or with **Move to folder…** in the row's **⋯** menu or under **Details** in Review. A recording is in one folder at a time, or in none.
- **Deleting a folder** never deletes a recording: what was in it (recordings and folders) moves up into the folder above it, and Memento asks first, saying where they go.
- Folders only sort the Library. The recordings' files stay where they are on disk, whatever you move.

**Dates.** A new recording is called "Untitled meeting · 2026 February 16" (or Untitled interview, and so on) until you give it a title. **Settings › General › Date format** chooses how dates are written there and everywhere else Memento shows a full date (Review, History, documents, dates in earlier years in the Library): 2026 February 16, 16 February 2026, February 16, 2026, 2026-02-16, or as Windows shows them. Titles you already have are not changed, and times stay as they are.

**Delete audio.** To free space but keep what was said, choose **Delete audio…** in a recording's **⋯** menu or under **Details** in Review. Memento asks first, naming the recording, how long its audio is and how much space it frees. The audio tracks and the mix are deleted; the transcript, documents, attachments and any video stay, and the recording stays in the Library to read. Its audio can't be brought back, so it can no longer be played, exported or transcribed again, and its History says when the audio was deleted. **Settings › Storage and history › Delete audio tracks older than** does it for you for recordings older than a day, a week, a month, 90 days, a year or two to five years (Never by default): it says how much it would free and asks before deleting anything that already exists, and then runs when Memento starts and once a day, never while you record or while a recording is being processed.

## Documents

In Review, choose **Create document**. The **document builder** opens with your default template. The **Template** list in its header holds the built-in templates (Meeting minutes, Interview notes, Lecture summary, Dictation clean-up) and then your own; choosing one opens it, and with unsaved changes Memento asks first. Going back with unsaved changes asks too: **Keep editing**, **Save template** (then leaves) or **Leave without saving**. **Save template** keeps the template you are on (a built-in is saved as a copy with its own name) and **Save as new template** always makes another one. Sections already on the template are greyed in the palette and marked "in use"; you can still add them again.

- **Sections**: drag sections from the palette into rows of up to three side by side: summary, decisions, action items with owner and deadline, agenda, discussion, open questions, quotes, timeline, next meeting, your own text, a section written to your own instructions, the full transcript and more. Give each one instructions and a length, choose whether it links its statements to the transcript, and set its text size. The preview beside it updates as you go.
- **Follow-up email**: a section that is an email ready to send to the people who were there: who it is to (the participants), a subject, a greeting, a short recap, the decisions and the action items with their owners, and a closing. Choose its **Tone** (Neutral, Warm or Brief: only the greeting, opening and closing change) and the name to **Sign off as**. Like every section, its recap, decisions and actions are checked against the transcript. In the viewer, **⋯ › Copy the follow-up email** puts just the email on the clipboard, without its heading or timestamps, ready to paste into Outlook or any mail program.
- **Inputs**: tick what the document may use: the transcript, the details, participants, the agenda, highlights, attachments and earlier documents. Audio and video are never used or sent.
- **Who writes it**:
  - the **local model** runs on your PC and sends nothing anywhere. Install it once in **Settings › AI and privacy** (Qwen3.5 4B for a graphics card, Ministral 3 3B for the processor). It is good for a first draft with citations and review. Twenty minutes of meeting take a few minutes on a laptop graphics card, much longer on the processor. The **larger models** (Qwen3.5 27B and Qwen3.5 35B-A3B, see Settings below) are much bigger, take much longer and need a lot of free memory; on Memento's test meeting they were not more accurate than Qwen3.5 4B, so try one on your own recordings before you rely on it;
  - **Claude** (Anthropic) or **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) with your own key. Turn on **Allow external AI services** and add the key in **Settings › AI and privacy** first. **Preview exactly what will be sent** opens a sheet that shows, before anything is sent, who it goes to and with which model, whether **Ask before every send** is on, each input with its tick and size, what Settings holds back, and every request word for word: the instructions the model gets and the part of the recording it reads. Search them with the field at the top, and **Copy this request** or **Copy everything** to keep a copy. With **Ask before every send** on (the default) Memento asks again before each send, naming the provider, what is included and how much.
  - **Your own server**: an OpenAI-compatible server you run yourself, such as Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM or llama.cpp's server, on this PC or on another computer on your network. Set its address and model in **Settings › AI and privacy › Your own server** (see [Your own server](#your-own-server)). A server on this PC writes like the local model: nothing leaves the PC, it needs no external AI and nothing is asked before it writes. A server elsewhere is treated like Claude or ChatGPT: it needs **Allow external AI services**, **What may be shared** applies, and **Ask before every send** asks first. The card in the builder names the model and the address ("qwen3:8b at localhost:11434 · on this PC"); when it is not ready it says why and offers the fix (**Set the address**, **Choose a model**, **Allow external AI services**). **Show live output** shows its reply arriving word by word, and **How this was made** and History name it with where it was: "Your own server · qwen3:8b at localhost:11434 (this PC)" or "(on your network)".
  - **Which Claude or ChatGPT model** writes is chosen in **Settings › AI and privacy**, under each key. Claude offers claude-opus-5-5 (the default, the most capable for long transcripts), claude-fable-5-1 (Anthropic's top tier: the newest and most intelligent, costs more and needs 30-day data retention on the account), claude-sonnet-5-5 (fast and strong) and claude-haiku-5-5 (the fastest and cheapest); ChatGPT offers gpt-6-astra (the default), gpt-6.1-sol and gpt-6-luna. **Other model…** takes any other model id the service offers, typed exactly as it names it; Memento marks it "not in Memento's list" and sends it without effort settings. The Builder's provider card, the preview, the confirmation, the Live output, the document's **How this was made** and History all name the model that was used. A long transcript is split into segments for every model, so none is too long for the model you chose. If the service does not offer the model (a typo, or one your account cannot use), the generation stops with "Claude does not offer a model called '…'", nothing is changed, and **Choose a model** takes you to the list; Memento never tries another model in its place.
  - **Your own server** (2.5.0): a model you run yourself in Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM, a llama.cpp server or any other server that speaks the OpenAI chat API. Memento needs its address (for example `http://localhost:11434` for Ollama or `http://localhost:1234` for LM Studio; a trailing `/v1` is fine), the model's name as the server lists it (for example `qwen3:8b`), a key only if the server asks for one, and the **context size** the server really gives the model: Ollama gives 4,096 tokens unless you raised `num_ctx`, so set that number, or raise it in Ollama and set the new one. Memento reads at most a quarter of it as transcript per request and splits a long transcript to fit. **Test connection** asks the server only for its model list (and Ollama for its version) and shows what it found. Where the server runs decides what leaves your PC: a server at `localhost` (or 127.0.0.1) is on this PC, so nothing leaves it and **Allow external AI services** is not needed, exactly like the local model; any other address, on your network or on the internet, receives the inputs you tick, so it needs **Allow external AI services**, asks before every send like Claude and ChatGPT, and gets only what Settings shares. Plain `http://` is accepted on this PC and your local network only; anywhere else use `https://`. How this was made and History name the model and the address and say "(this PC)", "(on your network)" or "(remote)".
- **Generate**. Every statement is checked against the part of the transcript it cites; what the transcript does not support is left out, and an owner or deadline is kept only if someone named it. A section the meeting never reached says "Not discussed."
- **Show live output**, beside **Cancel** while a document is written, opens a sheet where you can watch the exchange as it happens: on the left every step in order (the transcript cut into segments, each section read from each segment, the merge done in code, every check against the transcript, the final grounding check), on the right the selected step's request, exactly as sent, and the reply. The local model's reply appears word by word as it writes, with the number of tokens and tokens per second; Claude's and ChatGPT's replies arrive whole. The sheet follows the newest output; scroll up or pick a step to stop following, and **Follow live output** to go back. Close it and open it again at any time while the document is written; once it is done, **Show live output** in the viewer's side column shows the finished exchange until you leave the document. Nothing in it is sent anywhere or saved.

In the builder, **Undo** (or Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change: a section added, moved or removed, its instructions, length, text size, heading or link, the template's name, what the AI receives, the provider, the style or the output. Typing in one field is one step. While a text box holds changes you made since clicking into it, Ctrl+Z undoes your typing there first.

The **document viewer** then shows the result on paper. Click a timestamp to hear that moment, edit a paragraph in place ("Saved" appears when it is stored; **Undo** takes back a run of typing or a toolbar change, and a rename), regenerate with changed instructions, or go back to an earlier version: click a version's name under **Versions** to read it on the paper, read-only, then **Restore this version** (Undo takes it back) or **Back to current**. **How this was made** lists exactly what was used, what was sent and where, and what was checked and dropped; History keeps the same record. Read a generated document before you rely on it: the checks catch statements nobody made, but a summary can still put the weight in the wrong place.

**Styles** decide how documents look, never what they say: Corporate, Minimal, Academic, or your own copy with its typefaces, sizes, heading colour, spacing, Letter or A4 paper, page numbers and a running header. Manage templates and styles in **Settings › Documents**. Export a document as Word, PDF or Markdown from the viewer, or with the recording from the Export dialog.

## Export

In Review, choose **Export**. Tick what you need:

- the mixed audio, or each track, as FLAC, WAV or MP3;
- the video (when the recording has it) as **One file**, the screen with the camera picture where it was while recording and the mixed sound, or **Two files**, screen and camera each at their own size with the sound in the screen file. **MP4** encodes the picture again (about a third of real time or faster on a typical PC); **Original container** copies the recorded frames untouched in seconds, but cannot place the camera picture on the screen, so the camera comes as its own file;
- the transcript as JSON, Markdown, plain text or SRT subtitles (several at once);
- the recording's documents as Word, PDF or Markdown;
- the recording details, and the attachments.

For the Markdown and text transcript you choose, under the Transcript row, whether to include **Timestamps** and **Speakers**, and how to break it into **Paragraphs** (as each format always did, one paragraph per speaker turn, or line by line), in any combination. Memento remembers your choice. JSON keeps everything and SRT subtitles keep their times and speakers.

**Copy to clipboard**, next to Cancel, puts the transcript (as text or Markdown, with those choices) or one of the documents on the clipboard instead of writing files. A document pastes formatted into Word or Outlook, and as Markdown anywhere else. The document viewer's **⋯** menu has **Copy to clipboard** too.

The files go into a folder named after the recording, with a `manifest.json` that lists each file's size and SHA-256 fingerprint, so you can check later that nothing changed. Progress shows in the status footer. A folder Memento cannot write to is refused before anything is written. If an export fails, is cancelled, or Memento closes in the middle of it, the files it had written are removed; the recording inside Memento is never changed.

## Settings

Open Settings with the gear at the top right; it opens at the section you looked at last. Every change is saved at once. When another screen sent you here (Choose a model in the document builder, Open Settings › Transcription in Review), the back control at the top left names that screen and takes you back to it.

- **General**: theme (follows Windows by default), list density, **Date format** (see [The Library](#the-library-folders-dates-and-deleting-audio)), **Start Memento with Windows** (only for an installed Memento; it starts minimised, or only in the tray), **Keep running in the tray when closed** (off by default: with it on, closing the window leaves Memento in the notification area with Open, New recording and Quit, and a recording in progress shows on the icon and its tooltip; Quit ends Memento, and a recording in progress stops with everything so far kept), where the library lives (moving it copies and checks every file before removing the old copy), language, **Updates**, and **About** (version, library location and the licenses of the components inside Memento).
- **Recording**: the default sources and recording type, **Video defaults** (see [Recording video](#recording-video); record video by default, the default source and camera, resolution, frame rate, quality, the camera picture's corner, the graphics card's encoder and the mouse pointer), how often a checkpoint is saved, the storage format (lossless FLAC by default, or smaller AAC or MP3 files made after processing), **Keep only the mix** (off by default: after processing, each source's own track file is removed and only the mix is kept; speakers can then no longer be identified per track and tracks can no longer be exported for that recording, and its History says so),
  **Even out source levels** (on by default: when the mix is made, a quiet source such as a far-away microphone is lifted to the loudest source's speech level, by at most 24 dB, so you can hear everyone when you play the recording; each source's own track stays as recorded, and the transcript always hears every track at an even level whatever this setting says), and the free-space warning level (10 GB by default).
- **Transcription**: transcribe automatically or by hand, **Live transcript while recording** (see [During the recording](#during-the-recording); and whether it may use the graphics card), pause when the PC is busy, the model, the model to use without a graphics card, the language, word timings and the uncertainty mark. Under **Engine**, a line says how much of the graphics card's memory is free and which programs use the rest; when the model needs more than is free, it says so and what to close. **Check again** reads the card again.
- **Speakers**: identify speakers, the expected number, remember speakers by voice and the known voices (see [Speakers](#speakers)), and the speaker models.
- **AI and privacy**: the local models, with **Use this model** beside each installed one; under them **Larger models** (below) and a line that says which model writes documents, where it runs (graphics card or processor) and why (when only one is installed it is used, whatever the card has free), and beneath it the graphics card's free memory, who uses the rest and **Check again**; **Allow external AI services** (off by default) with what may be shared (Audio and Video are listed as never sent), **Ask before every send** and **Keep a record of what was sent**; the default provider; and your Claude and ChatGPT keys, which are encrypted for your Windows account and never shown again, each with the model it is sent: Memento's list with a note beside each model, the default first, and **Other model…** with a field for a model id typed by hand (letters, digits and . _ : / -, up to 100 characters) and **Use this model**; and **Your own server**, an OpenAI-compatible server you run (see [Your own server](#your-own-server)).
- **Documents**: the default template and style, **Manage templates and styles**, **Agenda photos and scans** (Windows OCR, or Tesseract once you install **Tesseract English** from its card here; Remove is off while Tesseract is chosen), and version history for transcripts and documents.
- **Export**: the default folder and what to include.
- **Storage and history**: how much space the library uses, its largest recordings, how much the separate tracks take, making older recordings smaller, **Keep only the mix for existing recordings** (asks first, says how much it frees, cannot be undone), **Remove video older than** (Never by default; it says how much it would free now), **Delete audio tracks older than** (Never by default; the same, for the audio: the transcript, documents and video are kept), and rebuilding the library list.

### Larger models

Settings › AI and privacy lists two larger local models under **Larger models**, below Qwen3.5 4B and Ministral 3 3B. They write on your PC like the other local models and send nothing anywhere, but they are much bigger, so they use the computer's RAM beside the graphics card:

- **Qwen3.5 35B-A3B**, a *mixture of experts*: it has 35 billion parameters but reads only about 3 billion of them for each word, so it is fast for its size. Memento keeps its "experts" in RAM and runs the rest on the graphics card. A 20.5 GB download; it needs about 22 GB of memory (RAM and graphics card memory together), on top of what Windows and your other programs use.
- **Qwen3.5 27B**, a *dense* model: every word reads all of it. Memento puts as many of its layers on the graphics card as fit and the rest in RAM, or runs it in RAM alone. A 15.6 GB download; it needs about 17 GB of memory. It is the slower of the two.

Each card shows the download size, the licence (Apache-2.0), the memory it needs and a line that says how Memento would run it on your PC right now, for example "Runs on the graphics card with the experts of 37 of 40 layers in RAM · about 12 tokens/s · 18 GB of RAM while writing · minutes of a 20-minute meeting in about 18 minutes". **Install** shows the size before it starts; a download that is interrupted continues where it stopped, and Memento checks first that the drive has room. Choose **Use this model** to write documents with it; the Builder's Local card then names the model and where it runs, and the document's **How this was made** says where it ran, how fast and how much RAM it took.

On a laptop with a 6 GB graphics card and 40 GB of RAM, twenty minutes of meeting take about 18 minutes with Qwen3.5 35B-A3B and about an hour with Qwen3.5 27B (2.5 minutes with Qwen3.5 4B); a model that has to run in RAM alone, because another program holds the graphics card, takes about an hour (35B-A3B) or several hours (27B). They need **at least 32 GB of RAM** to be useful; with 16 GB neither fits.

**When memory is short.** Memento works out where a larger model goes before every document, from the graphics card's free memory and the RAM free at that moment, and keeps memory for the rest: 2 GB for Windows, some for Memento itself, for a recording if one is running and for a transcription if one is waiting. If the model does not fit, the card says so instead of the plan ("Qwen3.5 35B-A3B needs 25 GB of memory; this PC has 19 GB free. Close other programs or choose a smaller model."), the Builder cannot generate with it, and Memento never switches to another model by itself: close programs you do not need, choose **Check again**, or choose a smaller model. If another program takes the graphics card's memory while a larger model writes, Memento moves more of the model into RAM and carries on, more slowly. If you start a recording while a larger model writes and the PC is short of RAM, the recording always comes first: the model is unloaded, the document is not changed, and History says so; generate it again when the recording has finished.

### Your own server

If you already run a language model server, Memento can write documents with it. Any server that speaks the OpenAI chat API (`/v1/models` and `/v1/chat/completions`) works: **Ollama**, **LM Studio**, **vLLM**, **llama.cpp**'s `llama-server` and others. Memento never starts, installs or changes the server; it only sends it requests when you generate a document with it.

Set it up in **Settings › AI and privacy › Your own server**, below the Claude and ChatGPT keys:

- **Address**: where the server listens, for example `http://localhost:11434` for Ollama on this PC, `http://localhost:1234` for LM Studio, or `http://192.168.1.20:11434` for Ollama on another computer at home. It is saved when you press Enter or leave the field. **Test connection** asks the server which models it has and says what answered ("Ollama 0.12.3 · 5 models"), or what went wrong ("Nothing answered at localhost:1234. Check that the server is running…"). Testing sends nothing but that question.
- **Model**: choose one of the models the test listed, or **Other model…** to type a model id exactly as the server names it (for example `qwen3:8b`). For Ollama, pull the model first (`ollama pull qwen3:8b`); for LM Studio, load it in LM Studio.
- **Context size**: how many tokens the server reads in one request, 8192 by default. Set it to what the server really runs: Ollama uses 4096 unless `num_ctx` was raised (in the model's settings or with `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH`); LM Studio shows it when you load the model. Memento splits a long transcript so each part fits in a quarter of this, so a larger context means fewer, longer requests.
- **Key**: optional. Ollama needs none. Add one only if your server asks for one, such as vLLM started with `--api-key` or a server behind a proxy that checks a key. The key is encrypted for your Windows account like the cloud keys, never shown again, and sent only to this server's address.

The line under the card says where the server is and what that means:

- **On this PC · nothing leaves it** (localhost, 127.0.0.1): it works with **Allow external AI services** off, and Memento does not ask before each send because nothing is sent anywhere.
- **On your network · needs Allow external AI services** (a private address such as 192.168.x.x or 10.x.x.x, or a computer's name on your network): what you tick leaves this PC for that computer, so it needs **Allow external AI services**, and **What may be shared** and **Ask before every send** apply as for Claude and ChatGPT.
- **Remote · needs Allow external AI services and https** (any other address): the same, and the address must start with `https://`. Memento never sends to a public address over plain `http://`, because a key or a transcript could be read on the way.

Audio and video are never sent to your own server either. If the server stops answering, does not have the model, asks for a key or does not answer like an OpenAI-compatible server, the generation stops, nothing is changed, and the builder says what happened and offers the fix (**Check the address**, **Choose a model**, **Add the key**). Memento asks for structured answers (a JSON schema) and falls back to plain JSON on servers that do not support them.

## Updates

Memento checks this project's GitHub releases for a newer version when it starts and then once a day, but never while you record or while a recording is being processed: it waits until both are finished. A new version downloads in the background (the footer shows the progress), and then a message offers **Restart to update**. Nothing is installed until you choose it; if you do not, the update is installed the next time Memento starts. Your recordings, settings and models are kept.

**Settings › General › Updates** shows your version and when Memento last checked, and has **Check now**. Check now only checks: it says "You are on the newest version", or that a newer version is available and shows that version's release notes. Nothing is downloaded until you choose **Update now**; the download then shows its progress, and **Restart to update** installs it. Turn off **Install updates automatically** if you do not want Memento to check by itself; **Check now** still works. A check sends nothing about you or your recordings: it is a plain request for the list of releases, the same as opening the Releases page in a browser.

## Where your data lives, and how to back it up

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Your recordings (the library) | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\Library` unless you moved it (Settings › General shows where) |
| Your folders | `folders.json` inside the library (the folder names only; which folder a recording is in is written in that recording's `project.json`) |
| Known voices (only with Remember speakers by voice on) | `voices\known.json` inside the library: voice signatures, never sound |
| Settings | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\settings.json` |
| Downloaded models | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\models` |
| Encrypted AI keys | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\secrets.bin` (readable only by your Windows account on this PC) |
| Logs and crash reports | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\logs` (no transcript text, no names, no keys) |
| The program itself | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\MementoApp` (replaced by updates; your data is never kept there) |

Type `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento` in the File Explorer address bar to open the folder.

Inside the library, each recording is a folder under `projects` with its tracks (`tracks\*.flac`, and `tracks\screen.mp4` and `camera.mp4` for video), the mix, the transcript, details, attachments and its history. The folders are complete on their own: `library.db` is only an index, rebuilt from them when needed. Library folders are not folders on disk: every recording stays in `projects` whatever folder you put it in. After **Delete audio** a recording's folder has no `tracks\*.flac` and no mix, only its transcript, documents and details.

**To back up**, close Memento and copy the whole library folder to another drive or a backup service. **To restore**, copy it back (or to a new PC) and, if it is not in the default place, choose that folder in Settings › General › Library location. Models can always be downloaded again; keys cannot be moved to another PC and must be entered again.

The default library is deliberately not in Documents, because Documents is often synced to OneDrive. If you move the library into a synced folder, your recordings will be uploaded by that service.

## When something goes wrong

- **Memento or Windows closed during a recording** (a crash, a power cut): at the next start Memento repairs the recording and shows **Recovered an interrupted recording**, with how much was saved, how many tracks are intact (video tracks included: every part of the video written before the crash plays) and what may be missing (at most a second or so after the last save). It never asks whether to keep it.
- **Memento closed while saving, transcribing or exporting**: saving and transcription continue where they stopped at the next start. A half-finished export is removed and History says so; export again from Review.
- **A device stopped**: only its track ends; the time is shown and the others continue.
- **The library's drive is not connected** (for example a USB drive): Memento says which folder and drive it is looking for and does not record until it is back. Plug the drive in and choose **Try again**, or choose another location in Settings › General.
- **A model file is damaged**: Memento checks every model before it uses it. A damaged file is set aside, and Review offers **Download … again**; transcription then continues by itself.
- **Transcription failed**: Review explains why and offers a fix. **Retry on CPU** works on any PC, just more slowly.
- **A quiet microphone**: in recordings made with versions up to 2.0.0-beta.1 a quiet microphone beside loud system audio (a video playing, a call) could be left out of the transcript entirely, because its speech never reached a fixed level. Now every track is judged against its own room noise and heard at an even level, so **Reprocess › Transcribe again…** in Review brings that speech back. If the microphone is also hard to hear when you play the recording, choose **More › Reprocess › Rebuild the mix with evened levels**: the mix is made again with the quiet source lifted to the others (the tracks themselves are not changed, and History says by how much). To go back, choose **Rebuild the mix as recorded**. For the next recordings, move closer or raise the microphone level in Windows (Settings › System › Sound › Input) when the Record screen says it is quiet.
- **Memento says another program is using the graphics card**: for example "The graphics card has 0.8 GB of 6 GB free. Ollama (llama-server.exe) is using 5.0 GB. Qwen3.5 4B needs 3.6 GB on the card, so it runs on the processor until that memory is free." The reading is the card's own (it matches what the NVIDIA driver reports); the memory is really in use, often by a program that starts with Windows and keeps an AI model loaded, such as Ollama or an app that bundles it (Memento then names that app: "started by …"). Nothing is lost meanwhile: transcripts and documents are still made, on the processor, more slowly. To use the card, close that program or unload its model (for Ollama, `ollama stop` followed by the model's name, or quit Ollama from the notification area), then choose **Check again** in Settings › Transcription or Settings › AI and privacy; no restart is needed. If the line names Memento itself, a transcription or document is running and the memory comes back when it finishes.

If something still goes wrong, the log in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Memento\logs` helps to find out why. It contains file paths and engine messages but no transcript text, names or keys.

## Privacy

Recordings, transcripts and everything else stay in the library folder on your PC. Memento connects to the internet only to:

- check this project's GitHub releases for updates and download them, unless you turn this off (see [Updates](#updates)). This is the only connection Memento makes by itself;
- download a transcription, speaker, text-recognition or local AI model you choose to install (from the addresses listed with each model);
- send the inputs you tick to Claude or ChatGPT, only after you allow external AI services and add your own key, and only when you generate a document with them. The local model sends nothing;
- send the inputs you tick to **Your own server** when you generate a document with it, and ask it for its model list when you press **Test connection**. A server at `localhost` is reached over the PC's own loopback address, so nothing leaves it; a server on your network or elsewhere is reached only after you allow external AI services, and never over plain http to a public address.

Audio and video are never sent anywhere, and video is never transcribed, analysed or used for a document (see [Recording video](#recording-video)). The live transcript and suggested chapters are made on your PC. Voice signatures for **Remember speakers by voice** stay on this PC in the library's `voices` folder and are never sent (see [Speakers](#speakers)). There is no account, no telemetry and no advertising.

The Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime, which draws Memento's window, has its own background connections to Microsoft (runtime updates and configuration). Memento cannot turn them off; they carry nothing from your recordings, because the window never loads anything from the internet.

## Keyboard

Everything can be reached with Tab and Shift+Tab; the focused control has a visible ring.

**Going back.** The Library is home; every other screen has a back control at the top left that names where it goes. It goes to the screen you came from when another screen sent you there (a document's timestamp opened Review: Back returns to the document; Regenerate opened the document builder: Back returns to the document; a link opened Settings: Back returns to that screen), and otherwise one step towards the Library (the document builder and a document go to their recording, Review and Settings to the Library). **Alt+←**, the keyboard's Back key and the mouse's back button do the same as the back control. **Esc** does too, once nothing else is open: it first closes an open menu, list, dialog or side panel, clears Review's filter or line selection and leaves fullscreen video, and only then goes back. Esc never goes back from the Record screen, while you are typing in a box, or from the document builder while the template has changes you have not saved (Alt+← still does, and the changes are dropped). Coming back to the Library keeps your search, type filter, sort, view and folder, and the recording you opened is selected.

On the Record screen, Space pauses and resumes and Ctrl+M marks a highlight; Esc does not stop a recording. In dialogs, Esc cancels. In Review, the document builder and the document viewer, **Ctrl+Z** undoes the last change and **Ctrl+Y** or **Ctrl+Shift+Z** redoes it; in a text box you have typed in, they undo and redo your typing first. In Review, F2 or Enter twice edits the focused transcript line, Esc shows every line again when the transcript is filtered, Ctrl+A, Space, Shift+arrows and Shift+F10 select lines and open a line's menu, and in the speaker menu the arrow keys, Enter and Esc choose. Memento follows Windows' "Show animations" setting: with animations off, buttons no longer move when pressed and the processing dot stops pulsing.

## Known limitations

- The live transcript is a rough draft from the Small model: it has no speakers, runs 10–20 seconds behind, and where a 10-second window cuts a sentence it can guess the rest wrongly. The full transcript after you stop gets it right.
- Importing a video file uses its sound only.
- Video was tried on a PC without a camera: camera recording and its thumbnail were tried with the OBS Virtual Camera (through DirectShow), but a webcam through Media Foundation, the camera light and the camera privacy messages follow Windows' documented behaviour and have not been tried with a real camera. The exact name of Windows' screen-capture privacy page has not been checked on a PC where it is turned off.
- Exporting video as MP4 encodes on the processor (about 70 frames per second for 1080p on the reference laptop, so an hour of 30 fps takes around 25 minutes); Original container takes seconds.
- **The larger models are slow and memory-hungry.** Qwen3.5 35B-A3B and Qwen3.5 27B have been measured on one laptop (RTX 3060 6 GB, 40 GB of RAM); other graphics cards and memory speeds will differ, and the speed Settings shows is an estimate until a document has been written with the model.
- **Generated documents are drafts.** The local model is good for a first draft with citations and review; check what it wrote against the transcript before you send it on. Claude and ChatGPT have so far been tried only against a test server, not with a real key.
- In a 4-hour recording made while another program kept the processor at 100% the whole time, the microphone track ended about 0.7 seconds out of step with the others. This was not seen without that outside load.
- There is no button to stop a transcription that is running; it pauses by itself while you record or the PC is busy, and continues where it stopped.
- Memento's transcription always lets other programs go first. A pass on the graphics card does not pause when the processor is busy, but while other programs keep every processor core fully busy it can stand still at the same percentage; it carries on once they are done.
- "Everything this PC plays" records the output device that was in use when the recording started. If you switch Windows to another output device mid-recording, sound sent to the new device is not on that track (an app source follows the app wherever it plays).
- Speaker identification can split one person into two speakers or merge two similar voices; rename, move or merge them in Review.
- English is the only interface language. Transcription detects the spoken language, or use the language you set in Settings.
- The installer and updates are not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen warns the first time. Compare the SHA-256 of a download with `SHA256SUMS.txt` on the release page to check it.
